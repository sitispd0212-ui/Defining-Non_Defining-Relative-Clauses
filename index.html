Grammar Material

Defining vs Non-Defining Relative Clauses

Essential Info No Commas

Defining Relative Clauses

Give essential information about someone or something: information that we need in order to understand what or who is being referred to.

Relative Pronouns Used:

who that which whose whom

Examples from Textbook:

"They're the people whom I met last night."
"Those are the houses that were flooded during the storm."
"The money was given to the boy whose bike was stolen."
Extra Info Uses Commas ( , )

Non-Defining Relative Clauses

Give extra information about a person or thing. It is not necessary information. Commas are used to set apart the non-defining clause.

Relative Pronouns Used (Note: "that" is NEVER used):

who which whose whom

Examples from Textbook:

"My best friend, who lives next door, is called Jimmy."
"The red car over there, which belongs to Dirk, is 10 years old."
"That woman, whom I met last year, is talking to her friend."

Interactive Meaning Simulator

Defining and non-defining relative clauses can sometimes look almost identical, but have completely different meanings! Click the buttons below to switch perspectives:

"My sister who works at the music shop has two cats."
Meaning: The speaker has more than one sister. The one he's talking about works at the music shop.
